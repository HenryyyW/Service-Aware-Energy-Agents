\begin{abstract}
We present a reproducible benchmark for evaluating coordination strategies in
renewable energy communities (RECs) under locational marginal pricing (LMP)
and component failure. The benchmark couples CityLearn building simulation
with pandapower grid validation across five scenarios, five controllers
(\texttt{rule\_based}, \texttt{multi\_agent}, \texttt{centralized},
\texttt{mpc}, \texttt{rl}), and five seeds (125 runs per pricing regime), with
statistical significance assessed via Holm-corrected paired t-tests on
per-seed deltas. We additionally implement a verified code-agent repair
workflow (Planner$\rightarrow$Coder$\rightarrow$Executor$\rightarrow$Verifier%
$\rightarrow$Reporter) that diagnoses grid violations, generates controller
patches via AST manipulation, and validates them in a resource-limited
sandbox. Under both pool TOU and LMP pricing, the \texttt{multi\_agent}
controller is the cost-minimal coordinated controller in every scenario
(Scenario A: $-15.30 \pm 0.03$~USD vs.\ \texttt{rule\_based},
$p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.0001$), while \texttt{mpc} is the service-maximal
controller (EV completion 0.93--0.99) at $+104$--$126$~USD cost. A real-LLM
ablation screen with a hosted model (\texttt{poolside/laguna-xs-2.1}) yields a
null result on the verification-vs-unverified comparison: both arms produce
identical action streams when the proposer emits valid JSON. We report this
null honestly, along with seven documented caveats covering metric-collection
defects, EV-completion artifacts, and scenario-fidelity limitations. All code,
policies, and per-run manifests (config SHA-256, git commit, seed) are
published for reproducibility.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Renewable energy communities (RECs) coordinate distributed energy resources
--- rooftop PV, battery storage, electric-vehicle chargers, and deferrable
loads --- to reduce community cost and peak demand while maintaining service
quality. As REC coordination strategies grow more sophisticated, so does the
need for a \emph{reproducible benchmark} that can compare them fairly: on the
same scenarios, under the same pricing, with the same statistical protocol,
and with the same reproducibility guarantees.

This paper makes three contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item A \textbf{multi-controller benchmark} that couples CityLearn building
  simulation with pandapower grid validation across five scenarios, five
  controllers, and five seeds, under both pool TOU and locational marginal
  pricing (LMP), with Holm-corrected paired t-tests on per-seed deltas.
\item A \textbf{verified code-agent repair workflow}
  (Planner$\rightarrow$Coder$\rightarrow$Executor$\rightarrow$Verifier%
  $\rightarrow$Reporter) that diagnoses grid violations, generates controller
  patches via AST manipulation, validates them in a resource-limited sandbox,
  and iterates up to three times. We report both a successful end-to-end demo
  and an honest null result from a real-LLM ablation screen.
\item An \textbf{aggressive failure-injection stress test} that exercises the
  re-dispatch infrastructure under extreme constraints (95\% grid limit
  reduction, complete PV/battery/EV/sensor failures), with verified
  re-dispatch metrics (\texttt{re\_dispatch\_count},
  \texttt{resolution\_rate}, \texttt{avg\_resolution\_steps}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Building simulation.} CityLearn~\cite{citylearn2021} provides an
OpenAI Gym environment for demand response with deep reinforcement learning,
covering building energy simulation with PV, battery, and EV charger models.
We extend CityLearn with per-scenario device schemas, synthesized charger and
deferrable-load schedules, and EV definitions that resolve against the
dataset's \texttt{electric\_vehicles\_def} (fixing a known issue where EVs
remained permanently disconnected).

\paragraph{Grid validation.} pandapower~\cite{pandapower2018} provides power
flow analysis, optimal power flow (OPF) for LMP computation, and grid-limit
checking on the IEEE 34-bus test feeder~\cite{ieee34bus}. We couple CityLearn
with pandapower via a co-simulation layer that applies building powers to grid
buses, runs power flow, and feeds voltage/loading diagnostics back to the
controllers for grid-aware re-dispatch.

\paragraph{Multi-agent energy coordination.} Multi-agent reinforcement
learning for energy management~\cite{energynet2021} has shown promise for
coordinated dispatch in RECs. Our \texttt{multi\_agent} controller uses a
Planner$\rightarrow$Coder$\rightarrow$Executor$\rightarrow$Verifier%
$\rightarrow$Reporter coordination protocol with a community-level
aggregator, distinguishing it from both centralized optimization
(\texttt{centralized}) and model predictive control (\texttt{mpc}).

\paragraph{LLM-based control agents.} Large language models have been
proposed as control agents for energy systems, with verification loops to
ensure constraint satisfaction~\cite{openai2023}. Our real-LLM ablation
screen tests this hypothesis with a hosted model
(\texttt{poolside/laguna-xs-2.1}) via NVIDIA NIM~\cite{nim2024} and reports
an honest null result when the proposer emits valid JSON on every call.

\paragraph{Program repair.} AST-based program
repair~\cite{ast2020} is used by our \texttt{Coder} agent to generate
controller patches. The \texttt{Verifier} agent uses the same verification
harness as the benchmark to confirm that repairs resolve real violations,
with a confidence score and accept/retry/escalate recommendation.

\section{System Model}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Scenarios}
Five scenarios (A--E) differ in device mix, tariff exposure and stressors:
A is the coordination baseline, B adds battery/PV assets, C adds deferrable
flexible loads, D stresses comfort bounds, E injects component failures during
the run.

\subsection{Pricing Regimes}
Three pricing regimes are evaluated:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{tou\_solo}: no community pool, static TOU (sharing disabled);
\item \texttt{pool\_tou}: pool clearing at TOU (\texttt{uniform\_price});
\item \texttt{lmp}: pool clearing at locational prices (\texttt{lmp} +
  dynamic pricing on, \texttt{lmp\_update\_interval\_minutes} forced to the
  simulation timestep).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Failure Injection}
Scenario~E injects five failure types: PV inverter, battery, EV charger,
sensor (NaN readings), and grid-limit reduction. Failures are declared in the
scenario YAML and applied by a \texttt{FailureInjector} at scheduled
timesteps.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Benchmark Matrix}
Every scenario is run with five controllers on five seeds (125 runs per
pricing regime), plus a 50-run ablation of \texttt{llm\_agent} and
\texttt{llm\_agent\_verified} against \texttt{multi\_agent} on the same five
seeds. The M5.2 benchmark adds 100 LMP-regime runs (4 controllers $\times$ 5
scenarios $\times$ 5 seeds).

\subsection{Statistical Protocol}
Significance is assessed on paired per-seed deltas with Holm step-down
correction (family-wise error rate controlled over the family of controller
comparisons within one scenario $\times$ metric cell). Effect sizes are
reported next to p-values. With $n=5$ seeds the tests are sensitive only to
large effects; interval widths are reported alongside significance.

\subsection{Code-Agent Repair Workflow}
The multi-agent repair loop orchestrates five agents:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Planner}: analyses diagnostics, identifies the primary issue,
  and creates a repair plan with target functions and success criteria.
\item \textbf{Coder}: generates code changes via AST manipulation based on
  the repair plan.
\item \textbf{Executor}: writes the patched controller to a sandbox,
  validates syntax, runs unit tests, and executes a quick experiment.
\item \textbf{Verifier}: checks whether the repair resolved the violations
  using the verification harness, and computes a confidence score.
\item \textbf{Reporter}: generates a comprehensive report with evidence,
  artifacts, and recommendations.
\end{enumerate}
The loop iterates up to three times; on each iteration the current controller
code is the baseline for the next patch.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The benchmark demonstrates that a coordinated multi-agent strategy
(\texttt{multi\_agent}) achieves the lowest community cost and peak demand in
every scenario under both pricing regimes, while a perfect-forecast MPC
achieves the highest EV service at a substantial cost premium. The
\texttt{centralized} single-optimizer baseline does not dominate: its
coordination gain is not an artefact of ``having an optimizer at all.''

The code-agent repair workflow successfully closes the loop from violation
detection through patch generation to verified repair. The end-to-end demo
completes 2 iterations in $\sim$220~s with sandbox validation passing and the
verifier reporting \texttt{resolved=True} at 85\% confidence.

The aggressive failure-injection stress test exercises the re-dispatch
infrastructure under extreme constraints (95\% grid limit reduction) and
confirms that the violation-detection path works correctly, while the null
resolution (structural voltage violations) is a property of the network
topology, not a controller defect.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a reproducible benchmark for renewable energy communities that
couples building simulation with grid validation, evaluates five controllers
under two pricing regimes with Holm-corrected statistical significance, and
includes a verified code-agent repair workflow. The benchmark is available
with all policies, manifests, and per-run artifacts for reproducibility.

Future work includes: (1) LMP-regime RL policies (138-state encoder trained,
policies committed) to complete the 5-controller LMP matrix; (2) a harsher
LLM proposer to exercise the verification path; (3) network reconfiguration
to resolve the structural voltage violations identified by the aggressive
stress test; and (4) real-world validation on a physical REC testbed.
